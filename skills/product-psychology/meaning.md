# Meaning — users invent a story

Dominant when they don’t know what this is, who it’s for, or what happens next.

### Social Proof
Unsure people copy similar others (Cialdini).
**Do:** real counts, “people like you,” most-common choice.
**Don't:** botted join numbers; popular filters that misfire (Airbnb).

### Scarcity
Limited supply raises value. Time / quantity / access.
**Do:** true limits (Uber Eats share-delivery window).
**Don't:** Sleepzy-style invented countdown. Fake scarcity → reactance.

### Curiosity Gap
Known vs wanted knowledge hurts (Loewenstein; Heath).
**Do:** open loops you will close (Morning Brew quiz).
**Don't:** blurry Tinder bait with no payoff.

### Mental Model
They already have a theory of how it works (Norman / Young).
**Do:** migrate from a known object (Trello card ← todo).
**Don't:** invent nouns (“workspace/instance/org”) on day one.

### Familiarity Bias
Known > new (mere exposure).
**Do:** Chromium-shaped Brave; Spotify podcast player = song player.
**Don't:** Snapchat-class redesigns with no bridge.

### Skeuomorphism
Real-world look/affordance eases new tech.
**Do:** Tesla battery as a phone battery; trash can.
**Don't:** leather texture with no new-tech job.

### Reciprocity
Give value before the ask (Cialdini).
**Do:** useful product *then* “make default.”
**Don't:** Brave-style default ask before they’ve seen the app.

### Singularity Effect
One identified person beats a group statistic.
**Do:** one cancellation story, not “16% are frustrated.”
**Don't:** fake composite customers presented as real.

### Variable Reward
Unpredictable reinforcement holds (Skinner VR; Eyal).
**Do:** mixed quality in a feed the user *chose*.
**Don't:** TikTok-class addiction loops without exit points.

### Aha! moment
First time they feel why they need it.
**Do:** instrument the action retained users do that churned users don’t; drive first session there.
**Don't:** celebrate signup. Signup is not aha.

### Goal Gradient Effect
Closer to the goal, faster (and endowed progress works).
**Do:** 2 stamps already on a 10-stamp card; trial timeline already started.
**Don't:** fake 90% complete that resets.

### Occam's Razor
Fewer assumptions usually wins.
**Do:** minimum info to finish the job; “how do I cut effort in half?”
**Don't:** delete a necessary decision and dump it on the user (Tesler).

### Noble Edge Effect
Genuine social care → loyalty (when believed).
**Do:** pause-for-hardship on cancel (Adobe missed this).
**Don't:** purpose-wash.

### Hawthorne Effect
Observed people change.
**Do:** assume diary studies and “we’re recording” warp behavior.
**Don't:** personalize so hard they fear exploring (TikTok rabbit-hole).

### Halo Effect
One trait spills to others (Thorndike 1920; “beautiful is good”).
**Do:** one excellent proof (support, polish) lifts the rest.
**Don't:** pretty security theater. Horn: one rude 404 taints the brand.

### Miller's Law
Working memory is tiny. Chunks, not “7 nav items.” Cowan ~4±1.
**Do:** chunk OTP/phone; don’t require holding a 12-step wizard in mind.
**Don't:** hide fees in “More” because of 7.

### Unit Bias
One presented unit feels like the right amount (Geier, Rozin, Doros 2006).
**Do:** smaller default units when less is the job (episode, scoop, credit).
**Don't:** autoplay the next unit; label a 24oz as one serving.

### Flow State
Challenge ≈ skill, clear goals, immediate feedback (Csikszentmihalyi).
**Do:** tune difficulty; kill popups mid-task.
**Don't:** call infinite scroll “flow.”

### Authority Bias
Costume of expertise (Milgram; Cialdini).
**Do:** relevant expert + clickable attestation.
**Don't:** white coat, fake SOC2, “official” phishing chrome.

### Pseudo-Set Framing
Arbitrary “set” demands completion (Barasz, John, Keenan, Norton 2017).
**Do:** 4 setup steps that are actually the user’s goal.
**Don't:** “3 of 4” that is an upsell.

### Group Attractiveness Effect
GD line = cheerleader effect: person/item looks better *in* a group.
**Do:** team / testimonial grids that are real.
**Don't:** stock mosaic to launder a weak offer.

### Curse of Knowledge
Can’t simulate not-knowing (Camerer et al.; Newton tapper 50% vs 2.5%).
**Do:** novice language; show the why.
**Don't:** “instance” as the first noun.

### Self-Initiated Triggers
User-authored prompts beat your spray (implementation intentions; owned triggers).
**Do:** “Remind me tomorrow,” add-to-calendar, custom Slack remind.
**Don't:** pre-ticked “set a reminder” that is just push harvest.

### Survey Bias
Answers skew polite / led / socially desirable.
**Do:** anonymous, balanced items, after the moment not in it.
**Don't:** “How much do you love…?” as a KPI.

### Cognitive Dissonance
Two opposing ideas hurt (Festinger). After they act, they rewrite beliefs.
**Do:** let them quit without identity attack.
**Don't:** “you’re not a quitter” on cancel.

### Feedforward
Consequence *before* the click (Djajadiningrat; Vermeulen CHI 2013).
**Do:** “Delete 12 photos,” “charge $X,” “email 8 guests.”
**Don't:** friendly label on a harmful side effect.

### Hindsight Bias
After the outcome, it looks inevitable (Fischhoff).
**Do:** log predictions before the launch/postmortem.
**Don't:** “users were always going to churn.”

### Law of Similarity
Same look → same group (Gestalt).
**Do:** one color for primary; one red for destructive.
**Don't:** ad that looks like a system button.

### Law of Prägnanz
Ambiguity resolves to the simplest whole.
**Do:** make the simple reading true (one charge, safe, done).
**Don't:** messy pixels that read as “free.”

### Streisand Effect
Hiding it advertises it (Masnick 2005).
**Do:** quiet correction, narrow scope.
**Don't:** theatrical takedown of lawful criticism.

### Spotlight Effect
They overestimate how much others notice them (Gilovich et al. 2000).
**Do:** private drafts, unlisted, empty-profile safety.
**Don't:** fake “X people are viewing your cart.”

### Fresh Start Effect
Temporal landmarks open new goals (Dai, Milkman, Riis 2014).
**Do:** align prompts with *their* Monday / birthday / job change.
**Don't:** “Fresh Start Sale ends tonight” as fake scarcity.
