# Memory — users keep a few bits

Dominant when they mis-remember the session, don’t return, or judge the whole by one moment.

### Provide Exit Points
Invite them to put it down when the job is done (Extra Credits; mindfulness design).
**Do:** Duolingo “you hit today’s goal — go.”
**Don't:** TikTok’s weak “maybe later” vs one more flick.

### Peak-End Rule
Judged by the affective peak and the ending (Kahneman).
**Do:** Zapier confetti after a hard pay; Tesla “you just finished charging.”
**Don't:** Adobe cancel that hides the confirmation and pushes more product.

### Sensory Appeal
More senses → stickier (HBR sensory marketing).
**Do:** sound as a Superhuman reward; food imagery that isn’t dry.
**Don't:** noise on every click.

### Zeigarnik Effect
Unfinished stays available (1927; Lewin tension).
**Do:** visible remaining daily goal.
**Don't:** open loops you never let them close.

### Endowment Effect
“Mine” is overvalued (Kahneman, Knetsch, Thaler).
**Do:** “your board,” saved books, named workspace.
**Don't:** pre-checked gifts that are charges.

### Chunking
Grouped bits beat a pile (Miller; this page’s 4 buckets).
**Do:** 5 nav links, 3 product groups (Stripe); modules not 106 orphans.
**Don't:** a wall of equal chips.

### Delighters
Unexpected play works *after* basics (Kano).
**Do:** Superhuman-class tiny joys.
**Don't:** delight on a broken checkout. Delighters decay into expected.

### Internal Trigger
A feeling/memory opens the product (Eyal).
**Do:** attach to a real job (airport → language practice).
**Don't:** engineer loneliness as the hook.

### Picture Superiority Effect
Pictures beat words via dual code (Paivio) if literal and seen long enough.
**Do:** product shot + label; unique icons.
**Don't:** replace text with abstract blobs; starving carousels.

### Method of Loci
Stable place helps retrieval (memory palace → spatial constancy).
**Do:** keep Settings / Logout / primary action where they were.
**Don't:** reshuffle chrome every A/B or hide exit.

### Shaping
Reinforce the next approximation, then raise the bar (Skinner).
**Do:** “create one note” before “invite the team.”
**Don't:** points for empty clicks; loot-box almost-rewards.

### Recognition Over Recall
Show it; don’t make them remember it (Nielsen H6; Norman knowledge-in-the-world).
**Do:** menus, recent, visible labels, password managers.
**Don't:** gesture-only first-run. Experts still want shortcuts (flexibility).

### Storytelling Effect
Stories beat fact lists (Bower & Clark; Green & Brock transportation).
**Do:** you arrived → tried X → got Y.
**Don't:** composite “customers” sold as real; tidy story over base rates.

### Negativity Bias
One bad outweighs several goods (Rozin & Royzman; Baumeister).
**Do:** repair the failure (apology + fix + better end).
**Don't:** fake “account at risk.”

### Availability Heuristic
Easy to recall feels common/likely (Tversky & Kahneman 1973). Not “prefer recent.”
**Do:** real rates + a concrete story.
**Don't:** cherry-picked logos as if they were the base rate. Asking for 12 reasons to stay can *lower* commitment (hard retrieval).

### Spacing Effect
Gaps beat cramming for later memory (Ebbinghaus; Cepeda).
**Do:** drip the second-session tip; come-back-tomorrow lesson.
**Don't:** 40-screen day-one tour, or spacing as a cover for spam.

### Serial Position Effect
First and last of a list win (Murdock; Glanzer & Cunitz).
**Do:** price / permission / logout at an end, not the soggy middle.
**Don't:** legal dump mid-scroll, Accept at the end. A modal after the last item wipes recency.
