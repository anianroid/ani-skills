# Information — users filter

Dominant when they skip, miss the CTA, or drown in options.

### Hick's Law
More options → slower, harder choice.
**Do:** cut, hide, or progressive-disclose. If you must keep many, familiar order + scannable groups.
**Don't:** 15-item dropdown at a commit step (Trello-style signup jobs).

### Confirmation Bias
They hunt for what they already believe.
**Do:** make the disconfirming fact unavoidable (price, permission, what will email).
**Don't:** filter bubbles that only show agreeing proof.

### Priming
The last stimulus tilts the next act.
**Do:** set the frame before the ask (privacy before search-engine choice; trip imagery before book).
**Don't:** prime fear you cannot resolve.

### Cognitive Load
Total mental effort to finish the task.
**Do:** one question per step when the job is commit; strip chrome at the moment of choice.
**Don't:** Mario-Kart home pages unless the clutter is the game.

### Anchoring Bias
First number/info dominates later judgment.
**Do:** show the honest reference first (annual as monthly; compare to status quo time).
**Don't:** luxury decoy price whose only job is to make the real price look small — unless the luxury offer is real.

### Nudge
Small cue, no force. Defaults, salience, priming, perceived variety.
**Do:** Airbnb-style “add dates?” as help.
**Don't:** Amazon-style default that is not in the user’s interest.

### Progressive Disclosure
Core now, power later.
**Do:** first-run shows the one path to aha.
**Don't:** hide fees, export, or cancel behind “simple.”

### Fitts's Law
Time to hit = distance + smallness.
**Do:** primary action large and near the thumb/cursor.
**Don't:** tiny “manage subscription” next to a huge “keep plan” (Adobe-class dark pattern).

### Banner Blindness
Ad-shaped or ad-adjacent stuff is unseen.
**Do:** put the money CTA in content rhythm, not banner real estate.
**Don't:** mix ads into the feed until everything is blocked.

### Decoy Effect
Asymmetrically dominated third option steers the pair.
**Do:** only if the decoy is a real SKU someone might want.
**Don't:** Economist “print only at print+web price” theater.

### Framing
Presentation moves the choice (Prospect Theory).
**Do:** Blinkist-style “hours you won’t reread.”
**Don't:** Tesla-style cost-only frame when the job is the benefit.

### Attentional Bias
Current goals/emotions steal gaze (dot-probe / threat).
**Do:** match the user’s current job in copy and chrome.
**Don't:** “account compromised” banners that are really upsells.

### Empathy Gap
Cold designer ≠ hot user (Loewenstein).
**Do:** write the 11pm failed-payment state, not the office calm.
**Don't:** one-click annual while dopamine is high without undo.

### Visual Anchors
Landing spots that stabilize the scan path. Not price anchoring.
**Do:** one face / one product / one button as the first lock.
**Don't:** equal-weight screens with nowhere to look.

### Von Restorff Effect
The isolate is remembered (1933).
**Do:** one primary among ghosts; one tinted plan.
**Don't:** badge everything. Color-only isolate fails a11y.

### Visual Hierarchy
Designed reading order via size/weight/position/space.
**Do:** squint test — primary still wins.
**Don't:** encode rank only in color or 11px legal.

### Selective Attention
Focus makes the rest invisible (gorilla / inattentional blindness).
**Do:** put errors and price in the current attentional set.
**Don't:** “they looked at it” from eye-tracking as proof they saw it.

### Survivorship Bias
You only study what came back (Wald’s planes).
**Do:** interview churn, bounced sessions, failed clones.
**Don't:** copy Airbnb because Airbnb survived.

### Juxtaposition
GD’s line is grouping (near + similar). Ads: side-by-side compare.
**Do:** before/after, price was/now, us vs them — if true.
**Don't:** glue “Accept” to an unrelated “Subscribe.”

### Signifiers
How you *discover* the affordance (Norman 2008).
**Do:** label the result (“Delete 12 photos”), visible click affordance.
**Don't:** whole-card looks clickable, only the 12px link is.

### Contrast
Attention follows weight; JND is a ratio.
**Do:** WCAG contrast; filled vs outline.
**Don't:** gray-on-gray “minimal” or motion-as-contrast only.

### External Trigger
Next step is inside the prompt (Eyal: paid/earned/relationship/owned).
**Do:** “Reset password” in the email; empty-state button that creates.
**Don't:** “you’re missing out” when nothing happened.

### Centre-Stage Effect
Middle of a *visible* set is chosen (Valenzuela & Raghubir: inferred popularity).
**Do:** put the genuine default/recommended in the center.
**Don't:** center the expensive plan and lie “most popular.”

### Law of Proximity
Near = related (Wertheimer).
**Do:** label flush to its field; padding inside a card > gap between cards.
**Don't:** price sitting closer to the wrong SKU.

### Tesler's Law
Irreducible complexity moves to user, app, or platform.
**Do:** spend engineering so the user does not pay a daily tax.
**Don't:** delete recovery/undo and call it simple.

### Spark Effect
Tiny effort → more action (Fogg Ability / Tiny Habits). Not Fogg’s Spark prompt.
**Do:** one photo, not “complete your profile”; one-click when they already want it.
**Don't:** “just one more easy click” into a subscription.

### Feedback Loop
After the act, close Norman’s gulf of evaluation.
**Do:** press → spinner → “Saved.” Error + next step.
**Don't:** silent save, infinite spinner, fake success.

### Expectations Bias
Priors color perception (umbrella: confirmation, placebo, mental model).
**Do:** match the promise you already made; don’t leak expectancy in tests.
**Don't:** “instant” marketing + 8s spinner.

### Aesthetic-Usability Effect
Pretty feels easier (Kurosu & Kashimura 1995; Tractinsky).
**Do:** invest in polish *and* measure task success.
**Don't:** let SUS on a pretty prototype replace completion rate.
